Privacy Policy

MacSalad · Last updated September 29, 2026

Support home Common questions

The short version

  • There is no MacSalad account, and no ads, analytics, tracking or third-party SDKs in the app. Your data is never sold.
  • Your Apple Health data never leaves your iPhone through MacSalad.
  • Your food diary syncs through your own private iCloud database. I (the developer) have no copy of it and can’t see it.
  • Two things leave your phone: the text of a meal you ask Sous Chef, MacSalad’s AI, to estimate online (through MacSalad’s server to an AI service, named below), and barcode or food look-ups (to Open Food Facts).
  • When Sous Chef estimates on your iPhone, with Apple’s on-device model, nothing leaves your phone at all.
  • MacSalad’s server does not store or log your meal text.

Who is responsible

MacSalad is made by Shayne Torres, an independent developer. Questions about this policy go to shayneptorres@gmail.com.

What the app works with

To do its job, MacSalad handles:

  • Your food diary — meals, foods, recipes, your goals, weekly schedule and flex days.
  • Meal descriptions — what you type or say when you describe a meal. Dictation is transcribed on your iPhone by Apple’s on-device speech recognition.
  • Camera images — barcodes and nutrition labels you scan. The barcode number is read on your iPhone, and label photos are read by Apple’s on-device model; the images themselves are not uploaded by MacSalad.
  • Apple Health data, with your permission — see Apple Health below.
  • Goal set-up answers — age, sex, height, weight and how active your week is, if you use the guided goals.

None of this is collected by me. It lives on your iPhone and, for the diary, in your own iCloud.

What stays on your device

  • Apple Health data. Everything MacSalad reads from or writes to Health.
  • Goal set-up answers. The calculator runs on your iPhone, and your answers are stored only on your iPhone — not in iCloud and never sent anywhere.
  • Drafts of meals you haven’t finished checking, a record of which meals have been written to Health, and your app settings.
  • On-device estimates. When a meal is estimated on your iPhone (the built-in portion table, or Apple’s on-device model), nothing about it leaves your phone.

Like most apps’ data, this is included in your iPhone’s own backups (iCloud Backup, or a backup to your computer), which are under your control and governed by Apple.

What leaves your device, and where it goes

Meal descriptions, to Sous Chef online

Sous Chef is MacSalad’s AI for estimating meals. It runs in one of two places: on your iPhone, using Apple’s on-device model, where nothing is sent anywhere; or online, where only your description is sent.

Who makes online estimates: online Sous Chef estimates are generated by an AI service from Anthropic, a third-party AI company. This is the only third-party AI service MacSalad sends your data to.

When online estimates are switched on and you describe a meal, MacSalad sends only the text of your description, your iPhone’s language and region code (for example en-US, so the answer comes back in the right units) and the time of the request to MacSalad’s server. The server passes the text to Anthropic’s AI service, which returns the estimate. No name, email, Apple ID, location, health data or diary is sent.

To stop the service being abused and to apply a daily limit, each request is signed using Apple’s App Attest: your iPhone generates an anonymous key for this installation of the app, and Apple vouches that the request comes from a genuine copy of MacSalad. That key identifies an installation, not a person.

Anthropic processes the text under its Commercial Terms of Service, which state that “Anthropic may not train models on Customer Content from Services.”

Online Sous Chef estimates are currently offered in the TestFlight beta, where they are on by default. You can switch them off at any time in Settings (in the beta, under Developer → Sous Chef estimates); Sous Chef then estimates on your iPhone instead, where your iPhone supports it.

Barcode and food look-ups, to Open Food Facts

When you scan a barcode, MacSalad sends the barcode number to Open Food Facts, a free public food database, to fetch that product’s nutrition label. When you search for a food by name, or tap to look up an estimated item, the search words are sent to Open Food Facts’ search service. Nothing about you (no account, diary or health data) is attached. Open Food Facts’ own privacy policy applies to those requests.

Your diary, to your iCloud

See iCloud below.

Things you choose to share

If you share a day, a meal or a recipe, it goes where you send it — Messages, Mail, AirDrop and so on. A shared recipe contains its ingredients and their nutrition only; a shared day contains the day’s totals and items, not your notes, targets or Health data.

The optional Siri shortcut

If you install the optional “Log Food” shortcut, it runs in Apple’s Shortcuts app and uses Apple’s cloud model, so the meal you describe to it is sent to Apple’s Private Cloud Compute under Apple’s privacy terms. The app says so where it offers the shortcut. Nothing is saved until you tap Save.

What MacSalad’s server keeps, and for how long

The server is a small service hosted on Cloudflare. It is there only to pass meal descriptions to Sous Chef’s online AI service safely and within a budget.

  • Your meal text is never stored and never logged. It is held in memory only for the few seconds it takes to get an estimate.
  • For each request it writes a log line with technical details only: the length of the text, how long the estimate took, its cost, the model and prompt version, token and item counts, the language and region code, and whether it succeeded. No text, no key and no identifier. These lines are not kept: the service is configured without log storage, so they can only be seen live as they happen.
  • For each app installation it keeps the anonymous App Attest public key with Apple’s attestation receipt, a signature counter, when it was registered, and a count of today’s estimates with the time of the last one. The day’s count resets daily; the key is kept while the service runs, so the installation doesn’t have to register again.
  • Service-wide, it keeps one running total of this month’s spending, and short-lived one-time codes used when an installation registers, which are deleted when used or after five minutes.

Cloudflare, as the host, handles the network connection (including your IP address) to deliver requests, under its own privacy policy. MacSalad’s server code does not read or record IP addresses.

Apple Health

With your permission, MacSalad:

  • writes dietary energy, protein, carbohydrate, fat, saturated fat, fibre, sugar, sodium, cholesterol and water for the meals you log, and reads those back to confirm what arrived;
  • reads your workouts (with their active energy and distance) to show them on Today;
  • reads your weight, height, date of birth, sex and step count only when you tap Fill from Health in the goal set-up. It never writes these.

Health data is used only to provide these features. It is not sent to MacSalad’s server, to the AI service behind Sous Chef, to Open Food Facts or to anyone else; it is not stored in iCloud by MacSalad; and it is never used for advertising or sold. The Apple Watch app reads your nutrition from Health on the watch and never writes to it. You can change or withdraw these permissions at any time in the Health app or in iOS Settings → Health.

iCloud

Your meals, foods, recipes, goals, weekly schedule and flex days sync between your devices through Apple’s CloudKit, in the private database of your own iCloud account — the same way Notes or Reminders do. That data is stored by Apple under your account and Apple’s privacy policy. I have no access to it, and there is no MacSalad server copy. If iCloud is off for MacSalad, your diary stays on this iPhone.

No ads, no tracking, no selling

MacSalad contains no advertising, no analytics or crash-reporting SDKs and no third-party tracking code, and it does not track you across other apps or websites. Your data is never sold, rented or shared for marketing.

If you choose to share diagnostics with app developers in iOS Settings, Apple may provide me with anonymous crash reports, which I use only to fix bugs. TestFlight feedback you send is delivered to me by Apple, including any screenshot and note you attach.

Children

MacSalad is not intended for children under 13, and I do not knowingly receive personal information from them. The guided goal calculator will not suggest calorie targets for anyone under 18. If you believe a child has used the app to send information, email me and I’ll help.

Your choices

  • Turn off online Sous Chef estimates in Settings (in the beta, under Developer → Sous Chef estimates). Nothing is sent to the AI service afterwards.
  • Don’t scan or search if you’d rather nothing go to Open Food Facts; you can enter any food by hand.
  • Limit Apple Health access in the Health app or iOS Settings → Health. MacSalad works without it.
  • Turn off iCloud for MacSalad in iOS Settings to keep your diary on this iPhone only.
  • Delete your data: swipe away today’s meals; delete the app to remove everything on this iPhone; delete MacSalad’s iCloud data from your iCloud storage settings; and delete its Health data in the Health app (your profile → Apps → MacSalad).

Because MacSalad’s server stores no meal text and nothing that identifies you, there is nothing on it that I can look up or hand over for you. The anonymous installation record described above cannot be linked to you by me.

Changes and contact

If this policy changes — for example, if online Sous Chef estimates come to the App Store version — the updated version will be posted on this page with a new “last updated” date. Questions about privacy? Email shayneptorres@gmail.com.
