What it is

MacSalad is a food diary that does the math for you. Type or say what you ate — “two eggs and toast with butter” — scan a barcode, or photograph a nutrition label, and it works out the calories, protein, carbs and fat. You check the numbers before anything is saved, and everything you log is written to Apple Health alongside your steps and workouts.

For iPhone, iOS 27 or later, with an Apple Watch app. No MacSalad account, nothing to sign in to, no ads and no tracking. Your diary syncs through your own iCloud.

Features

  • Describe it. Type or dictate a meal in plain words and get each food with its calories and macros, ready to check. In the beta, Order Up lists each food as you say it.
  • Scan it. Scan a barcode to look the product up in Open Food Facts, or photograph the nutrition label when there is no barcode.
  • Pick it. Save foods and recipes to your library, and tap your usual items for this time of day to add them again.
  • Apple Health. Calories, protein, carbs, fat and related nutrients go into Apple Health as you log them. Today also shows your workouts from Health for context.
  • Guided goals. Answer a few quick questions and MacSalad suggests calories and macros, with a line on why each number is what it is and the full arithmetic if you want it. Or type your own.
  • A weekly schedule and flex days. Give some days more than others while the week stays the same, and set aside flex days that are logged as usual but not scored.
  • Trends and a daily recap. After a few days, Trends shows your averages and how often you landed on your goal. A short recap of yesterday appears once a day, and can be turned off.
  • Widgets, Siri and Apple Watch. Calorie and macro widgets for the Home and Lock Screens, Shortcuts actions for logging by voice, and a watch app that shows your day.

How the estimating works

When you describe a meal, MacSalad breaks it into foods and estimates each one. Common counted foods (“two eggs”) come from a table of portions built into the app. Everything else is estimated by a language model: in the beta, Anthropic’s Claude when you are online and it is switched on, or Apple’s on-device model on iPhones that support Apple Intelligence.

An estimate is an estimate. Portion sizes, recipes and restaurant dishes vary, and a model can be wrong. MacSalad always shows every item and its numbers for you to check and edit before anything is saved, and it flags a food where no portion was given. If the macros don’t add up to the calories, it says so and offers a fix.

Scanned products come from Open Food Facts, a free, community-built database. Its data is only as good as the entry for that product, so it is worth a glance too.

Common questions

Is my health data uploaded anywhere?

No. What MacSalad writes to and reads from Apple Health stays on your iPhone — the app never sends Health data to me, to Anthropic or to anyone else. The answers you give in the goal set-up (age, height, weight and so on) are worked out on your iPhone and stored there. Your diary syncs through your own private iCloud, which I can’t see. The full detail is in the privacy policy.

What gets sent to Claude?

Only the words of the meal you described, plus your iPhone’s language and region setting so the answer comes back in the right units. No name, no account and no health data. It goes through MacSalad’s own small server, which does not store or log the text, on to Anthropic. Under Anthropic’s commercial terms, Anthropic may not train its models on it. In the beta you can switch Claude off in Settings → Developer → Estimate with Claude.

Does it work offline?

Mostly. Your diary, library, goals and Trends all work without a connection, and iCloud catches up when you are back online. Describing a meal offline uses the built-in portion table and, on iPhones that support Apple Intelligence, Apple’s on-device model. Barcode look-ups and food searches need the internet, because they ask Open Food Facts.

Why does it need Apple Health?

Apple Health is where your nutrition ends up, so it sits beside your steps, workouts and anything else you track. MacSalad asks for permission to write calories, protein, carbs, fat and related nutrients, and to read them back so it can check what arrived. It reads workouts to show them on Today, and reads your height, weight, age, sex and steps only when you tap Fill from Health in the goal set-up. You can use MacSalad without Health; your meals are simply not sent there.

How are my calorie and macro goals worked out?

Resting energy uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation. A typical day adds an activity factor (from the FAO/WHO/UNU 2004 report), then walks and workouts are added using the Compendium of Physical Activities. Your goal — lose fat, lose fat and build muscle, maintain, or build muscle — adjusts the total, and protein is set per kilogram of body weight (1.6 or 2.0 g/kg) with fat and carbs sharing the rest. The app shows every step under How we got here, and you can change any number it suggests.

Is this medical advice?

No. In the app’s own words: “This is not medical advice. A general estimate does not account for pregnancy or breastfeeding, medical conditions, medication that affects appetite or weight, or a history of disordered eating. Talk to your primary care provider before making changes to how you eat.” The guided calculator won’t suggest targets for anyone under 18.

What are flex days?

Days you set aside — a birthday, a trip, a long dinner. A flex day is logged and sent to Apple Health as usual, but it isn’t scored: Trends, streaks and the recap leave it out. Mark today as flex from the sparkles button on Today, or a day up to a week back from that day’s page. You choose how many you get a month on the Goals page; it starts at 4.

Can I give some days a bigger goal than others?

Yes. In Settings → Your goals → By day, spread the same week differently — more at the weekend, a little less on the other days. The week’s total stays the same, and Today names the day’s goal when it differs.

Why can’t I edit a meal from an earlier day?

On purpose. Past days stay as they were logged, so your Apple Health history doesn’t shift underneath you. Today’s meals can be edited or deleted at any time.

How do I delete my data?
  • A meal from today: swipe it away on Today. It is removed from Apple Health too.
  • Everything on this iPhone: delete the app.
  • Your synced diary: in iOS Settings, tap your name → iCloud → manage your storage, and delete MacSalad’s data. (The exact wording varies between iOS versions.)
  • What’s in Apple Health: in the Health app, open your profile → Apps → MacSalad, and delete its data.
Is there a Pro version?

Not yet. Everything is free while MacSalad is in beta. Any paid option will be announced here and in the app before it arrives.

Privacy

No MacSalad account, no ads, no analytics and no tracking. Your Apple Health data never leaves your iPhone through MacSalad, and your diary syncs through your own iCloud. Beyond that, MacSalad sends two things off your phone: a meal description you ask Claude to estimate, and barcode or food look-ups to Open Food Facts.
